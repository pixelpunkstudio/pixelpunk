Christmas Word Search Games: 8 Easy Ideas for Family Gatherings

October 21, 2026 · By Pixel Punk Studio

Seasonal ideasActivity ideas

  2. Make it a game, not a test
  3. Free Christmas puzzles to print

Big family Christmas gatherings have a familiar problem: the kids are restless, the grandparents can't join the video games, and everyone is waiting for dinner. A Christmas word search solves it better than almost anything: no rules to explain, no board to set up, and every generation can play at once. All you need is a printer, a few pencils and one good idea for how to play. Here are eight.

8 Christmas word search games

  1. The Christmas Eve race. Print the same puzzle for everyone, say "go", and the first to find all the words picks the Christmas movie. Give younger players a head start of three words.
  2. Team colors. Pair a child with a grandparent and give each team one puzzle and two pencil colors. The child spots, the grandparent circles, and everyone sees who found what.
  3. The Advent countdown. Slip one puzzle a day into an Advent calendar or an envelope by the tree. A themed puzzle each morning makes the wait for Christmas part of the fun.
  4. Placemat puzzles. Put a puzzle and a pencil at each place while the roast is in the oven. It keeps hungry guests happily busy, and it is a natural conversation starter.
  5. Read-aloud search. One person reads the word list slowly, everyone searches at the same time, and the first to call "found it!" gets a point. Great for larger groups and senior centers, because players never need to read the word list themselves.
  6. The carol challenge. Use a puzzle full of carol words: each time someone finds a word, the table sings the line it comes from.
  7. Stocking stuffer surprise. Roll a printed puzzle with a pencil and a ribbon and tuck it into a stocking or a Christmas card. It costs nothing and keeps someone busy on Christmas morning.
  8. The quiet hour. Not every game needs a winner. After the presents, put out a stack of puzzles and a pot of cocoa, and let everyone solve at their own pace.

Make it a game, not a test

  • Match the puzzle to the player. Large print puzzles for grandparents, easier puzzles with fewer words for young children, so everyone can actually finish.
  • Keep the solutions handy. Nobody should spend Christmas stuck on the last word. Print the answer page separately and hand it over when someone asks.
  • Use clipboards or trays. They turn any sofa or lap into a table.
  • Reuse your puzzles. Slide a page into a clear sheet protector and use a dry-erase marker: wipe it clean and the next player can start again.
  • Mix in a story. When someone finds a word like "sleigh" or "gingerbread", ask who has a memory about it. The puzzle becomes the start of the conversation.
