Stuck on Stocking Stuffers? How to Choose the Right Puzzle Book for Each Person on Your List

November 18, 2026 · By Pixel Punk Studio

A puzzle book is one of the easiest stocking stuffers there is: it costs little, it suits almost every age, and it keeps someone happily busy long after the wrapping paper is gone. The hard part is choosing. On the shelf they all look alike, yet the wrong one ends up in a drawer: letters too small for grandma, puzzles too easy for a teen, pages too detailed for a six-year-old. A few minutes of thinking about the person, not the cover, makes all the difference.

Four questions before you buy

  1. What do they already enjoy? Word searches, coloring, sudoku or crosswords. A new theme is a nice surprise; a new kind of puzzle is a gamble.
  2. How are their eyes? Someone who needs glasses for the newspaper will be happier with large print, and someone who uses a magnifier needs jumbo print.
  3. How much challenge do they want? Some people solve to relax, others want to be stuck for a while. Look at the grid size and the number of words, not only the age on the cover.
  4. Will it fit the stocking? Most large print books are 8.5 x 11 inches and won't slide into a small stocking. Stand it behind the stocking with a bow, or wrap it and let the pencils go inside.

Who gets which book

  • Grandparents with tired eyes: a large print word search with dark letters and single-sided pages.
  • Anyone with low vision: jumbo print, with letters of 24 points or more and fewer words per puzzle.
  • A parent who needs to unwind: a bold and easy coloring book, with big shapes that finish in one sitting.
  • A teen: bigger grids, words hidden backwards and diagonally, or a sudoku book that gets harder as it goes.
  • A child of 4 to 8: big, simple coloring pages, or a kids' word search with short words and no backwards ones.
  • The whole family: one book left on the coffee table, so anyone can add a word between visits.

Make it a complete stocking stuffer

  • Two good pencils with erasers, or a pencil sharpener shaped like something fun.
  • A highlighter in a soft color, for word searches.
  • Colored pencils or fine markers for a coloring book.
  • A clip-on book light for solving in bed or in a dim corner.
  • A small lap board or clipboard, so the book can be used from an armchair.
  • A handwritten note saying which puzzle you want them to solve first.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting "large print" alone: check the letters in the "Look inside" pages or the reviews.
  • Buying "for adults" for a child: long words and backwards ones frustrate early readers.
  • Double-sided coloring pages for marker fans: ink bleeds through and ruins the next picture.
  • A theme they don't care about: a cozy Christmas book suits most people in December; a niche hobby book only suits fans of that hobby.
